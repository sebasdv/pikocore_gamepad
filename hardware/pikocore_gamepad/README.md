# pikocore_gamepad

A new, independent handheld sampler/mangler — same purpose as the pikocore port in
this repo's root, but built from scratch around the **Waveshare RP2350-Plus** core
module with its own carrier PCB, not the RP2350-PiZero + GamePi13 HAT the rest of
this repo targets.

- **Hardware design & BOM:** [`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-11-pikocore-gamepad-hardware-design.md`](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-11-pikocore-gamepad-hardware-design.md)
- **GPIO pin map (source of truth: `pcb/pinmap.py`):** [`docs/hardware/pikocore_gamepad-pinout.md`](../../docs/hardware/pikocore_gamepad-pinout.md)
- **PCB pipeline plan & execution notes:** [`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-11-pikocore-gamepad-pcb-fork.md`](../../docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-11-pikocore-gamepad-pcb-fork.md)
- **NAV5 D-pad + speaker relocation (later layout change):** [`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-11-pikocore-gamepad-nav5-speaker-reloc-design.md`](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-11-pikocore-gamepad-nav5-speaker-reloc-design.md) · [plan](../../docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-11-pikocore-gamepad-nav5-speaker-reloc.md)

## Firmware target — `PIKO_GAMEPAD=ON`

```
mkdir -p build-gamepad && cd build-gamepad
cmake -DPICO_SDK_PATH=../pico-sdk -DPIKO_GAMEPAD=ON ..
make -j4
```

`PICO_BOARD` becomes `waveshare_rp2350_plus_16mb` (from the Pico SDK). The flag
reuses the whole GamePi13 port (12-button input layer, LCD UI, USB bank loader)
and swaps only what the carrier PCB changes:

- **Pins:** [`src/boards/pikocore_gamepad/config.h`](../../src/boards/pikocore_gamepad/config.h)
  (`PIN_*`, generated by hand from `pcb/pinmap.py`, kept in sync by gate G-4) mapped
  onto the shared `GAMEPI_*` names in `src/hw_gamepi13.h`. All 12 buttons use
  `gpio_pull_up()`; the D-pad is four discrete tacts (SW1–SW4).
- **Audio:** I2S to the PCM5102A over PIO0 (`src/gamepad/i2s.pio`,
  `src/gamepad/audio_i2s.h`), BCK GP16 / LRCK GP17 / DIN GP8 at 48 kHz. The
  audio engine still ticks off a PWM slice (slice 0, no GPIO routed) exactly as the
  PWM build does; a DMA channel in endless mode zero-order-holds its latest sample
  into the I2S FIFO. `DAC_XSMT` (GP22) stays low until clocks run and the first
  samples are out, then goes high. `SPK_SHDN` (GP20) follows `JACK_DET` (GP21,
  pull-up): plug in = speaker off.
- **LCD:** same vendored ST7789 driver on SPI0 (SCK GP2, MOSI GP3, DC GP5, RES GP6,
  BLK GP7 PWM straight to the module's BLK pin, 1 = on). The display is the 1.3"
  240x240 IPS module with a 7-pin header; it has no CS line (tied to GND on the
  module: `GAMEPI_LCD_CS_PIN -1`, skipped in `dev_shim.c`) and no TE pin, so GP26
  is free.
- **MIDI:** over the module's USB-C only. The board has no MIDI jacks since
  2026-11-05, so GP27 and GP28 are free and the one-wire receiver is not created.
- **Not built:** microSD (no socket; mode 8 stays unreachable, FatFs is not linked)
  and the HDMI mirror. `PIKO_GAMEPI13=ON` keeps building unchanged.

**Not bench-tested.** There is no assembled board yet: the target compiles, gate
G-4 passes, and the I2S/mute/jack sequencing follows the PCM5102A and PAM8302A
datasheets, but nothing above has run on hardware. First things to check on a
real board: DAC lock and unmute click, speaker/jack switching, the LCD SPI clock
(`GAMEPI_LCD_SPI_HZ`, left at the GamePi13's 10 MHz), and button polarity.

## `pcb/`

A scriptable KiCad generation pipeline (Python: `pinmap.py`, `netlist.py`,
`gen_sch.py`, `gen_pcb.py`, `checks/`), forked and adapted from a sibling project
(`GAMESETUP`) that targets the same RP2350-Plus module.

**Current state (layout of 2026-10-17, MIDI jacks removed on 2026-11-05):** **100 × 60 mm**,
4 layers, 66 parts: the Micro layout on the RP2350-Plus module
([spec](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-pikocore-gamepad-module-micro-midi-design.md)),
with the display switched to the 1.3" 240x240 ST7789 IPS module with a 7-pin
2.54 mm header ([spec](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-17-pantalla-7-pines-design.md)).
MIDI goes over the module's USB-C
([spec](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-05-usb-midi-audio-design.md)): the
two TRS jacks and their circuit (J6, J7, U9, U10, R40–R43, D3, C56) left the
board on 2026-11-05, GP27 and GP28 are free, and no other part moved.
The module (J4, 27.78 × 39.22 per the manufacturer's drawing) sits on the front
over its header, pins soldered by hand, pin row at the top; D-pad and X/Y/A/B on either side, Select/Start at the lower right,
triggers in the top corners. It carries its own backlight driver: `LCD_BLK` (GP7) goes straight to
its BLK pin. On the back the RP2350-Plus is **soldered flat by its castellated
edges** (footprint `RP2350-Plus_Castellated`) at (50, 33) with its USB-C at the
bottom edge. The phones jack is on the left edge. Hand copper: the boost loop
only. Gates: 216 tests OK; `run_all.py` exit 0
with every gate green, G-0 included ("all physical constants and footprints are
verified"), plus G-5/G-4 (23 pins)/G-2 (215 connections)/placement; ERC 0; DRC 0
errors, 0 unconnected, 0 parity mismatches, 11 warnings (silkscreen, and a
library mismatch on SW9/SW10). `gen_fab.py` without `--force` writes
`pikocore_gamepad_fab.zip` (150 KB, no NO-FABRICABLE suffix; 38 BOM lines, 17
without LCSC; 47 SMD in the CPL) and G-F reports everything up to date. The 17
lines without LCSC are parts the user buys and solders or places by hand (12
tacts, the phones jack, the speaker, the two 470 uF, U1 and J4); U1 and
J4 are not in the CPL. The three 22 uF (C5, C6, C28) use LCSC C602037, the same
Samsung series as C45783, which had no stock on 2026-11-05; C602037 may be
charged as an Extended part (up to US$3.07 per order), so switch back to
C45783 if it restocks before ordering. Gates passing is not the same as a board built or
checked: see the open items below. The DXF template (100 × 60) now carries J4 with its full module
outline, for the enclosure window. The display module has no 3D model, so it
does not appear in renders (the user's STEP, `pcb/1.3 Inch 1.3.step`, is in
the repo but not wired to J4). No board has been built.

**Why not the cheaper RP2040-Plus 16MB (evaluated 2026-11-04):** same Pico
footprint, pinout and battery header, US$1-2 less, but it does not fit. The
firmware runs from RAM (`copy_to_ram`, inherited from upstream pikocore): the
`PIKO_GAMEPAD` build configured with `-DPICO_BOARD=waveshare_rp2040_plus_16mb`
compiles, but the link needs 392 860 B of RAM against the RP2040's 262 144 B
main region (overflow of 130 716 B; code plus constants alone are 249 KB, so
even dropping the 115 KB framebuffer would not do). It only links as an XIP
build (151 092 B of RAM), and that is a port, not a board swap: audio ISR at
~1 MHz from the 16 KB XIP cache, flash writes during USB sample loading with
core 1 running from flash, and `src/gamepad/audio_i2s.h` uses the RP2350-only
DMA ENDLESS mode (an RP2040 needs two chained channels). The RP2350-Plus stays.

The user measured the module in hand and confirmed on 2026-10-17
`LCD7_PIN_ROW_TO_EDGE` = 1.27 mm and `LCD7_PIN_DRILL` = 1.0 mm; both are
`verified=True` in `pcb/params.py`, which is why G-0 is green.

Orientation: screen up, header row at the top, the leftmost label must read
GND and goes into the square pad (the board has GND/BLK silk labels). A wrong
orientation swaps GND/BLK, 3V3/DC and SCK/RES. The user's photo of the module
(2026-10-17) shows exactly this order (GND VCC SCL SDA RES DC BLK, left to
right, header on top, GND on the square pad), and the user confirmed on
2026-10-17 that the part in hand is the one in the photo: orientation is
closed.

Not verified (no gate covers it): the Plus's castellation pad pattern against
the Pico footprint (the user confirmed on 2026-10-17 that they are identical)
but not that its underside is flat so it sits on the board; the module's
battery connector against the user's JST GH cells (the carrier cannot reach the
module's battery line, so the cells need a GH-to-MX1.25 pigtail or a re-crimped
plug); MIDI over USB (firmware, never run on hardware); the BLK polarity and
pull state
of the display module (firmware assumes active-high); an optional 1 uF at J4
pin 2; the enclosure. When ordering, JLCPCB must be told not to assemble U1 and
J4 and not to paste U1's B.Paste-only openings.

To reproduce the board, `bash pcb/route_cycle.sh` regenerates schematic, netlist
and board from scratch (`gen_pcb.py` calls `NewBoard()`: GUI edits and the
previous routing are discarded) and re-routes with freerouting 2.5.0, with its
fanout stage off (it put vias against U1's hand-soldered pads); edit the JAR
path at the top of the script for your machine.

**History:** this layout reached `main` on 2026-11-04 through PR #3 (module
soldered flat, Micro layout, MIDI) and PR #4 (7-pin display module); the MIDI
jacks left it on 2026-11-05 (branch `hw/sin-jacks-midi`). Earlier
variants live in the history: the same board with the two MIDI jacks (`main`
before the `hw/sin-jacks-midi` merge), the same board with the bare 24-pin panel on a
flex (`hw/modulo-micro-midi` before PR #4 was merged into it), the 100 × 80 mm
board with the RP2350-Plus on a socket (`main` before merge `572ca8a`), and
the chip-down RP2350A board (branch `hw/chipdown-rp2350`, PR #2, kept as
reference).

`PARTES.md` and `PENDIENTES.md` inside `pcb/` are inherited from GAMESETUP and
describe *that* project, not this one — kept for the component research they
contain (verified footprints, part-sourcing decisions), marked historical at the
top of each file.
